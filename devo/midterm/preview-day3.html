Day 3

God Is Still Our Sanctuary

Ezekiel 11:14–21

Ezekiel ministered during one of the darkest periods in Judah's history. The nation had turned away from God, its leaders were corrupt, and many of God's people had already been carried into exile. Their circumstances were far worse than simply experiencing an unfavorable change in government. They had lost their homes, their security, and much of what was familiar. Yet in the middle of their hardship, God gave a remarkable promise. In Ezekiel 11:16, He told the scattered Israelites that He would be “a little sanctuary for them in the countries where they have gone.” Think about that. They were far from Jerusalem and the temple, but they were not beyond God's presence or His care. God also promised Israel a future restoration and a renewed heart. Those promises belong to Israel; they are not a guarantee of national restoration for America. But they reveal something beautiful about God's character. God's faithfulness is not dependent upon our circumstances. Sometimes we experience the Lord's presence most deeply when everything familiar has been stripped away. The situation may not change immediately. The hardship may continue. But God has not abandoned His people. And through Jesus Christ, we can draw near to Him wherever we are.

Key Hebrew Word Study

Sanctuary — Miqdāsh – A holy place, sanctuary, or place set apart for God's presence.

Application: God assured the scattered Israelites that they were not beyond His care, even while separated from their homeland and temple. For believers today, this reminds us of God's faithfulness and the privilege of drawing near to Him through Jesus Christ.

Practical Challenge

•Bring a difficult circumstance before the Lord and ask what He may be teaching you through it rather than focusing only on how quickly it can change.
•Spend intentional time alone with God, remembering that your fellowship with Him isn't dependent upon a particular location or favorable circumstances.

Reflective Questions

•Have I mistaken difficult circumstances for evidence that God has forgotten or abandoned me?
•Is the Lord using a season of uncertainty to draw me into a deeper relationship with Him?

Prayer

Lord, thank You that Your faithfulness never changes. Even when life feels uncertain or unfamiliar, help me draw near to You and find my refuge, strength, and comfort in Your presence. Amen.

Theological Challenge

How can we learn from God's promises to Israel in Ezekiel 11 without incorrectly applying those specific national promises to the church or to America?
