Day 1

A Kingdom That Cannot Be Shaken

Hebrews 12:28

Elections have consequences. Decisions made by those in authority can affect our families, communities, freedoms, and the future of our nation. As Christians, we should care about these things and take our responsibilities as citizens seriously. But there's something we must never forget: Our hope isn't found in a political party, an elected official, or a favorable election. Our hope is in Jesus Christ. Hebrews 12:28 reminds us that we are receiving a kingdom that cannot be shaken. Political power changes hands. Nations rise and fall. Laws change, and circumstances can become uncertain. But King Jesus remains, and His kingdom is secure. That doesn't mean we ignore what's happening around us. It means we keep an eternal perspective while living faithfully in the present. When things don't go the way we hoped, we have an opportunity to examine where we've placed our confidence. Have we become more concerned with preserving our comfort than fulfilling God's calling? We can be concerned without being consumed. Elections may change our immediate circumstances, but they cannot change our eternal future in Christ. And because His kingdom cannot be shaken, neither should our commitment to serving Him.

Key Greek Word Study

Shaken — Saleuō – To shake, disturb, agitate, or cause something to become unstable.

Application: Earthly governments and circumstances are subject to change, but God's kingdom is unshakable. Our confidence rests in the eternal reign of Jesus Christ rather than the temporary stability of this world.

Look Up Supporting Scripture

  • Psalm 20:7
  • Psalm 146:3–5
  • Philippians 3:20
  • Daniel 2:44

Practical Challenge

  • Identify something in the news or in your circumstances that has been consuming your attention. Intentionally spend that time in prayer and God's Word instead.
  • Thank the Lord for the eternal security you have in Christ, regardless of what happens politically or personally.

Reflective Questions

  • When circumstances don't go my way, does my response reveal that I've placed too much confidence in something temporary?
  • Am I more focused on preserving my comfort in this world or serving the King whose kingdom cannot be shaken?

Prayer

Lord, thank You that Your kingdom is eternal and Your promises never change. Help me be a responsible citizen without allowing the circumstances of this world to consume my heart or distract me from serving You. Amen.

Theological Challenge

How does our heavenly citizenship change the way we respond to political uncertainty without removing our responsibility to be faithful citizens?

Day 2

Perfect Peace in Uncertain Times

Isaiah 26:3–4

Have you ever noticed how quickly your peace can disappear when you begin focusing on everything that's going wrong? Maybe it's the latest election results, financial concerns, a troubling headline, or uncertainty about what tomorrow might bring. The more attention we give those things, the more anxious we can become. But Isaiah 26:3 gives us a wonderful promise: “You will keep him in perfect peace, whose mind is stayed on You, because he trusts in You.” Notice where that peace comes from. It isn't the result of favorable circumstances. It comes from keeping our minds fixed on the Lord. That doesn't mean pretending everything is fine or ignoring the challenges before us. It means remembering God in the middle of our reality. Jesus taught the same principle in Matthew 6. Rather than allowing tomorrow's concerns to consume us, He tells us to seek first God's kingdom and His righteousness. We don't know what tomorrow holds, but we know the One who holds tomorrow. So do what you can today. Be responsible. Be faithful. But entrust the outcome to the Lord. Your circumstances may not line up the way you hoped, but your heart can still be aligned with God. The mission hasn't changed.

Key Hebrew Word Study

Peace — Shalom – Peace, completeness, welfare, well-being, or wholeness.

Application: Isaiah describes a peace rooted in trusting the Lord. This peace doesn't require the absence of trouble. It comes from a heart and mind continually resting in God's faithfulness.

Look Up Supporting Scripture

  • Matthew 6:31–34
  • Philippians 4:6–7
  • John 14:27
  • Psalm 46:1–3

Practical Challenge

  • When anxiety begins to rise, stop and pray through Isaiah 26:3–4, intentionally turning your attention back to the Lord.
  • Replace some of your news or social media consumption this week with time in Scripture, prayer, or worship.

Reflective Questions

  • Am I spending more time feeding my fears than feeding my faith?
  • What circumstances have I allowed to determine my peace rather than trusting the Lord?

Prayer

Father, forgive me for allowing circumstances to consume my thoughts and steal my peace. Help me keep my mind fixed on You, trusting Your faithfulness even when I don't understand what lies ahead. Amen.

Theological Challenge

How can a believer experience genuine peace in Christ while still facing fear, uncertainty, and difficult circumstances?

Day 3

God Is Still Our Sanctuary

Ezekiel 11:14–21

Ezekiel ministered during one of the darkest periods in Judah's history. The nation had turned away from God, its leaders were corrupt, and many of God's people had already been carried into exile. Their circumstances were far worse than simply experiencing an unfavorable change in government. They had lost their homes, their security, and much of what was familiar. Yet in the middle of their hardship, God gave a remarkable promise. In Ezekiel 11:16, He told the scattered Israelites that He would be “a little sanctuary for them in the countries where they have gone.” Think about that. They were far from Jerusalem and the temple, but they were not beyond God's presence or His care. God also promised Israel a future restoration and a renewed heart. Those promises belong to Israel; they are not a guarantee of national restoration for America. But they reveal something beautiful about God's character. God's faithfulness is not dependent upon our circumstances. Sometimes we experience the Lord's presence most deeply when everything familiar has been stripped away. The situation may not change immediately. The hardship may continue. But God has not abandoned His people. And through Jesus Christ, we can draw near to Him wherever we are.

Key Hebrew Word Study

Sanctuary — Miqdāsh – A holy place, sanctuary, or place set apart for God's presence.

Application: God assured the scattered Israelites that they were not beyond His care, even while separated from their homeland and temple. For believers today, this reminds us of God's faithfulness and the privilege of drawing near to Him through Jesus Christ.

Look Up Supporting Scripture

  • Psalm 46:1–3
  • James 4:8
  • Hebrews 4:14–16
  • Hebrews 13:5–6

Practical Challenge

  • Bring a difficult circumstance before the Lord and ask what He may be teaching you through it rather than focusing only on how quickly it can change.
  • Spend intentional time alone with God, remembering that your fellowship with Him isn't dependent upon a particular location or favorable circumstances.

Reflective Questions

  • Have I mistaken difficult circumstances for evidence that God has forgotten or abandoned me?
  • Is the Lord using a season of uncertainty to draw me into a deeper relationship with Him?

Prayer

Lord, thank You that Your faithfulness never changes. Even when life feels uncertain or unfamiliar, help me draw near to You and find my refuge, strength, and comfort in Your presence. Amen.

Theological Challenge

How can we learn from God's promises to Israel in Ezekiel 11 without incorrectly applying those specific national promises to the church or to America?

Day 4

Faithful in the Middle of the Chaos

Ruth 1:1–5; Galatians 6:9

The book of Ruth begins during the days of the judges, a time when Israel was experiencing tremendous spiritual and moral decline. And right in the middle of that national turmoil, we're introduced to a family facing its own personal tragedies. There was famine, displacement, and eventually the deaths of Naomi's husband and two sons. Things were not going well. Yet as the story unfolds, we see God working through ordinary people who chose to remain faithful. Ruth couldn't fix the spiritual condition of Israel. But she could remain faithful to Naomi and follow the Lord. Boaz couldn't change the nation's direction. But he could treat people with kindness, generosity, and integrity. And while they were simply being faithful with what God had placed before them, the Lord was accomplishing something far greater than they could see. Ruth and Boaz would become part of the family line leading to King David and ultimately Jesus Christ. You may not be able to change what's happening in Washington, D.C., or Boise, but you can be faithful with what's in front of you. Love your family. Encourage someone who's discouraged. Pray for a friend. Serve your neighbor. Share the gospel. Your faithfulness matters, even when you cannot see the results. Stay the course. The mission hasn't changed.

Key Greek Word Study

Faithfulness — Emunah – Faithfulness, steadfastness, firmness, or reliability.

Application: Although this particular Hebrew word is not used in Ruth 1:1–5, it describes the quality of steadfast faithfulness we see throughout Ruth's story. God works through ordinary acts of obedience, even when the larger circumstances seem overwhelming.

Look Up Supporting Scripture

  • Ruth 2:11–12
  • Matthew 1:5–6
  • Luke 9:23
  • Colossians 3:23–24

Practical Challenge

  • Identify one ordinary responsibility or relationship where God is calling you to be faithful, and give it your attention today.
  • Encourage, pray for, or serve someone this week without expecting immediate results or recognition.

Reflective Questions

  • Am I allowing circumstances beyond my control to distract me from the responsibilities God has placed directly before me?
  • Do I believe that God can accomplish something eternally significant through my ordinary acts of faithfulness?

Prayer

Father, thank You that You are always at work, even when I cannot see what You're doing. Help me remain faithful in the ordinary things You've placed before me, trusting You with the results. Amen.

Theological Challenge

How does God's providence work through the ordinary choices and faithfulness of His people without removing their personal responsibility to obey Him?

Day 5

The Mission Hasn't Changed

2 Corinthians 5:18–20; Matthew 28:18–20

When circumstances become uncertain, it's easy to become distracted by everything happening around us. We can spend so much time following the news, debating political issues, or worrying about the future that we begin neglecting what Jesus has actually called us to do. But here's the truth: The mission hasn't changed. We're still called to love the Lord with all our heart, soul, mind, and strength. We're still called to love our neighbors, speak the truth in love, make disciples, and share the hope of the gospel. And that includes people who disagree with us. Jesus didn't tell us to love only those who share our values or political opinions. He even commanded us to love our enemies. People are not the obstacle to our mission. People are the mission. Paul reminds us in 2 Corinthians 5 that God has entrusted us with the ministry of reconciliation. We are ambassadors for Christ, representing our Savior to a world that desperately needs Him. That calling doesn't depend upon who's in office or what laws are passed. So don't allow disappointment to produce bitterness or fear. Let it draw you closer to Jesus and renew your commitment to the gospel. Elections may change the direction of a nation, but they don't change our Savior, His promises, or His purpose for our lives. The mission hasn't changed.

Key Greek Word Study

Ambassadors — Presbeuō – To serve as an ambassador or representative on behalf of another.

Application: As ambassadors for Christ, we represent Jesus rather than ourselves. Our words, attitudes, and actions should point people toward reconciliation with God, even when we face opposition or disagreement.

Look Up Supporting Scripture

  • Mark 12:29–31
  • Matthew 5:43–48
  • 1 Peter 3:15
  • 1 Timothy 2:1–4

Practical Challenge

  • Pray for those in authority, including leaders you disagree with, asking God to work in their hearts and give them wisdom.
  • Look for an opportunity to demonstrate Christ's love to someone this week through encouragement, forgiveness, kindness, or sharing the gospel.

Reflective Questions

  • Am I more eager to win an argument than to win someone to Christ?
  • Has frustration or fear made me less gracious toward people who need to experience the love of Jesus?

Prayer

Lord Jesus, thank You for entrusting me with the ministry of reconciliation. Keep my heart from bitterness, fear, and distraction, and help me faithfully represent You wherever You've placed me. Amen.

Theological Challenge

How can Christians stand firmly for biblical truth while demonstrating Christlike love toward those who disagree with them?

Closing Encouragement: Stay on Mission

We don't know everything the future holds for our country. There may be challenges, disappointments, and changes that affect our daily lives.

But we know the One who holds our future.

God is still saving people. He's still restoring lives, strengthening marriages, caring for the hurting, and drawing people to Himself.

And He wants to use us in that work.

So stay close to the Lord. Keep opening His Word. Keep praying, worshiping, and fellowshipping with other believers.

Be responsible with the opportunities and freedoms God has entrusted to you, but don't carry the burden of outcomes that belong to Him.

Be faithful with what's in front of you, and trust God with the results.

Elections may change the direction of a nation, but they cannot change our Savior, His promises, or the mission of His church.

The mission hasn't changed.
