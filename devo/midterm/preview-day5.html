Day 5

The Mission Hasn't Changed

2 Corinthians 5:18–20; Matthew 28:18–20

When circumstances become uncertain, it's easy to become distracted by everything happening around us. We can spend so much time following the news, debating political issues, or worrying about the future that we begin neglecting what Jesus has actually called us to do. But here's the truth: The mission hasn't changed. We're still called to love the Lord with all our heart, soul, mind, and strength. We're still called to love our neighbors, speak the truth in love, make disciples, and share the hope of the gospel. And that includes people who disagree with us. Jesus didn't tell us to love only those who share our values or political opinions. He even commanded us to love our enemies. People are not the obstacle to our mission. People are the mission. Paul reminds us in 2 Corinthians 5 that God has entrusted us with the ministry of reconciliation. We are ambassadors for Christ, representing our Savior to a world that desperately needs Him. That calling doesn't depend upon who's in office or what laws are passed. So don't allow disappointment to produce bitterness or fear. Let it draw you closer to Jesus and renew your commitment to the gospel. Elections may change the direction of a nation, but they don't change our Savior, His promises, or His purpose for our lives. The mission hasn't changed.

Key Greek Word Study

Ambassadors — Presbeuō – To serve as an ambassador or representative on behalf of another.

Application: As ambassadors for Christ, we represent Jesus rather than ourselves. Our words, attitudes, and actions should point people toward reconciliation with God, even when we face opposition or disagreement.

Look Up Supporting Scripture

•Mark 12:29–31
•Matthew 5:43–48
•1 Peter 3:15
•1 Timothy 2:1–4

Practical Challenge

•Pray for those in authority, including leaders you disagree with, asking God to work in their hearts and give them wisdom.
•Look for an opportunity to demonstrate Christ's love to someone this week through encouragement, forgiveness, kindness, or sharing the gospel.

Reflective Questions

•Am I more eager to win an argument than to win someone to Christ?
•Has frustration or fear made me less gracious toward people who need to experience the love of Jesus?

Prayer

Lord Jesus, thank You for entrusting me with the ministry of reconciliation. Keep my heart from bitterness, fear, and distraction, and help me faithfully represent You wherever You've placed me. Amen.

Theological Challenge

How can Christians stand firmly for biblical truth while demonstrating Christlike love toward those who disagree with them?

Closing Encouragement: Stay on Mission

We don't know everything the future holds for our country. There may be challenges, disappointments, and changes that affect our daily lives.

But we know the One who holds our future.

God is still saving people. He's still restoring lives, strengthening marriages, caring for the hurting, and drawing people to Himself.

And He wants to use us in that work.

So stay close to the Lord. Keep opening His Word. Keep praying, worshiping, and fellowshipping with other believers.

Be responsible with the opportunities and freedoms God has entrusted to you, but don't carry the burden of outcomes that belong to Him.

Be faithful with what's in front of you, and trust God with the results.

Elections may change the direction of a nation, but they cannot change our Savior, His promises, or the mission of His church.

The mission hasn't changed.
